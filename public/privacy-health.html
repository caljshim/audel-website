Consumer health data privacy policy

Effective October 24, 2026

This policy covers the consumer health data that Caleb Shim ("we", "us") collects through Audel, as required by Washington's My Health My Data Act and similar laws in Nevada and Connecticut. It applies to everyone who uses Audel. Questions and requests go to support@audel.ai.

The health data we collect, and why

  • Data you allow Audel to read from Apple Health for a goal you connect to it: steps; exercise minutes; active energy burned; workouts (the kind of workout, when it started and ended, and the app that recorded it); walking and running, cycling, swimming, wheelchair and downhill snow sports distances; sleep; mindful minutes; mood (State of Mind); resting heart rate; heart rate variability; weight; time in daylight; and nutrition: calories, protein, carbohydrates, total fat, fiber, sugar, water and alcoholic drinks.
  • Health goals and logs you enter yourself, such as a weight target, a sleep or mood check-in, or a meal you log, including the nutrition values Audel looks up for it.
  • Progress photos you add to a goal's logs, which can show your body. Audel doesn't use face recognition on them or create face templates or other biometric identifiers from them.
  • Your journal, if you use it: the mood you choose for a day (from awful to great) and the note you write. We treat both as health data, because mood is about your mental health.
  • What you tell the assistant about these things in a conversation.

We use this data only to show your progress toward the goals you set, to remind you about them, to answer you when you chat with the assistant and, only if you turn on Include chats about health, to review those conversations to improve Audel. Your journal is used only to show it back to you and, when you ask the assistant about it or ask it to log something, to answer you. Progress photos are used only to show them back to you, on their logs and side by side over time. We store it in Audel's database (progress photos, in a private Google Cloud Storage bucket), process it on our servers to work out your progress, and show it back to you in the app. We don't use it for advertising, don't sell it, don't use it to train AI models, and leave conversations involving it out of the copies we review to improve Audel, unless you choose to include them (see Your consent). Chats about health are reviewed only if you choose Include chats about health. A health detail you mention in a conversation that's otherwise about something else, such as money, may still reach those copies if the automated health check misses it.

Where it comes from

  • Apple Health on your iPhone, one kind of data at a time, for the goal you connect it to.
  • You, when you enter a goal, a log or a message.
  • Open Food Facts, which gives us the nutrition values of a packaged food you look up. We send it only the barcode or search words, nothing that identifies you.

Who it's shared with

We share health data only with these service providers, which process it for us, and only for what each is listed for:

  • Google Cloud, which hosts our servers, database and storage in the United States, stores all of it.
  • Google (Gemini) and Anthropic (Claude), the AI providers whose models write the assistant's replies: when you chat with the assistant, the one writing the reply receives the health data that reply needs, such as your progress toward a health goal, or the journal entries it reads when you ask about your journal or ask it to log something. A progress photo reaches them only when you open it and tap Ask Audel about this, and then only that photo. They process it for us under their commercial API terms, which don't allow training on your data.
  • Google (Gemini), for the review's health check: before a conversation of someone who hasn't chosen Include chats about health is copied for review, Gemini reads it once to check for health data, and the conversation is left out unless the check is confident it holds none.
  • Anthropic (Claude), for conversation review, only if you choose Include chats about health: your conversations that involve health data, without your name or email, which we read with Claude's help to find where the assistant got something wrong (see Improving Audel). Even with it off, a health detail you mention in a conversation that's otherwise about something else can reach those copies if the health check misses it.
  • Brave, which runs the assistant's web searches: when it looks something up for a goal, such as the nutrition of a food, the search words are sent to Brave Search and can concern your health. Nothing that identifies you is sent with them.
  • TypeSafe, which reads your answer when Audel asks you to confirm an action, with a short summary of that action, such as logging a meal to a nutrition goal, to tell whether you said yes or no.
  • Open Food Facts, which receives the barcode or search words of a packaged food you look up, and nothing that identifies you.
  • Apple's push notification service, which delivers Audel's notifications to your iPhone, including their text, which can name a health goal or routine and your progress on it.

These service providers process it for us, not for themselves. The AI providers, Brave and TypeSafe may process what they receive outside the United States, under their own terms and safeguards. Apart from them, we don't share health data, except when the law requires it, and we have no affiliates that receive it. We never sell it. No third party collects health data about your activity across other apps or websites through Audel.

Your consent

Before Audel reads anything from Apple Health, it shows you a Health data notice that says which data it will read, what it's for, who it's shared with, and how to stop it, and then Apple's own permission screen. Audel reads only what you allow there. You can withdraw your consent at any time: turn off Audel's access in the Health app (tap your picture, then Apps, then Audel) or in your iPhone's Settings, or remove the goal that uses it. Audel then stops reading that data. To delete what was already collected, see below.

Before Audel saves health data you enter yourself for the first time (a journal entry, a meal, a weight, sleep or a workout logged by hand, a health goal, or a progress photo, including anything you ask the assistant to log), it asks you first, in an iPhone alert: "Allow Audel to save your health data?", which says what's saved and why and that you can turn it off in Settings. Until you tap Allow, nothing is saved, and our servers refuse to save it. Only after you allow saving, a second alert asks separately whether to let Audel's AI use your health data, and says it goes to Audel's AI providers only when you ask about it; it stays off unless you tap Allow there. Without it, Audel still saves and shows your health data, but health goals, your journal and progress photos aren't sent to the AI providers, the assistant doesn't search the web for health goals, and notifications about a health goal don't name it. We record each choice, the version of the text you saw and when. You can change any of them at any time: open your profile, tap Account & sign-in, and find Health data under Privacy. Turning saving off stops new health data being collected, Apple Health included, and lets you keep or delete what's already saved. Earlier versions of the app asked on a Health data screen instead; update Audel to see the alerts.

Your journal and other health data reach the AI providers only after you agree to them on the app's How Audel uses AI page, which tells you that your messages and the data each reply needs are sent to them. You can take that agreement back at any time: open your profile, tap Account & sign-in, and switch off Let Audel use its AI providers.

Conversations that involve health data are left out of the conversation review unless you choose Include chats about health: a switch under Improve Audel with my conversations (open your profile, then tap Account & sign-in). It is off unless you turn it on. Before it turns on, Audel shows you what's included, why, who reads it and how to stop, and records that you agreed and which text you agreed to. You can turn it off at any time; conversations from then on are left out again, and copies already made are deleted within 30 days. Photos are never included.

Your rights, and how to use them

You can ask us to:

  • Confirm whether we collect, share or sell your health data, and access it, including a list of every third party and affiliate we've shared it with;
  • Correct health data that's inaccurate;
  • Delete your health data;
  • Withdraw your consent to our collecting and sharing it.

Email support@audel.ai from the address on your Audel account. You can also delete your whole account, and all your health data with it, in the app: open your profile, tap Account & sign-in, then Delete account. You can review and change your own goals and logs in the app at any time, and delete a day from your journal: open the day and tap Delete this day.

We'll confirm the request comes from you, by matching it to your account, and answer within 45 days, at no charge. If we need more time, we'll tell you why within those 45 days, and take no more than 45 days more. When we delete your health data, we delete it from our database and storage, and copies in our backups expire within 7 days. Our AI providers don't keep what they receive to train models, but may keep it in their abuse and safety logs for the limited periods their terms set before deleting it; conversation review copies are deleted within 30 days.

If we decline your request, reply to our decision to appeal. We'll answer your appeal within 45 days and explain our decision. If we deny your appeal, you can contact your state Attorney General: in Washington, at atg.wa.gov/file-complaint.

Geofencing

We don't set up a geofence around any place that provides in-person health care. Audel's place goals use a geofence only around a place you choose, and places that provide health care, such as hospitals and pharmacies, can't be chosen. The place's coordinates stay on your iPhone.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top and tell you in the app before the change takes effect. We won't collect, use or share health data in a new way, or for a new purpose, without telling you first and getting your consent.
